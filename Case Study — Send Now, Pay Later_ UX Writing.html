Case study — UX Writing

Teaching a new credit feature to people sending money home

How I wrote the copy for "Send Now, Pay Later" inside a money transfer app, from first banner to error states, so customers could understand a new offer without losing trust or momentum.

One

The problem

A global money transfer company partnered with a lending provider to let customers in Australia borrow a short-term top-up and add it to their transfer. The business wanted three things: a higher average transfer amount, more frequent transfers, and a value-added service customers would come back for.

The writing problem: a credit product appearing in the middle of a money-sending flow. People are cautious about fees and fine print when they send money. If the copy felt pushy or unclear, they'd ignore it or abandon the transfer.

Two

Research

  • Competitor analysis. I reviewed three similar services (Rewire, Xayo, Wagepay) to see how they describe fees, eligibility and repayment, and where they place the offer.
  • Information priority. With the designers, I decided what customers must see on each screen and what could wait one tap later.
  • Vocabulary testing. I tested terms with Australian users. Words like boost and top-up felt familiar and low-pressure, while heavier lending terms felt risky. I used the terms users already knew throughout the flow.

4. Interstitial: explain

Tapping Learn more opens the detail. I wrote the credit process as four plain steps, put the fee up front, and added one line on where the funds go, so there are no surprises later.

Five

Results

After launch, the offer saw a clear increase in adoption. We tracked it across the funnel:

  • Adoption rate increased after launch, as more customers chose to add a top-up to their transfer.
  • Banner click-through grew, especially on the home page, where the copy introduced the offer for the first time.
  • Average transfer amount was higher for customers who used the top-up, which was a core goal of the project.
Six

What I'd carry forward

  • Familiar words beat precise ones. The vocabulary testing changed several terms.
  • Error and edge states carry the most trust, because that's when customers check whether a brand is being straight with them.
  • Match tone to the moment. The same offer needed four different voices across four screens.
  • Next, I'd test banner wording and placement to push adoption further.
