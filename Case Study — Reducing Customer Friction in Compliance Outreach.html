Case study — Tone & Voice Redesign

Reducing Customer Friction in Compliance Outreach

How a full rewrite of 30 customer-facing compliance email templates cut back-and-forth, lowered support volume, and made a mandatory process feel less like an accusation.

One

The problem

The compliance team relied on roughly 30 templated email macros in Salesforce to request additional information from customers during identity and risk review — things like proof of residency, enhanced due diligence checks, and anomaly investigations. These templates had been built up over several years by different contributors, and it showed. Three issues stood out:

  • Inconsistent tone of voice. Some emails read as neutral instructions, others as formal legal notices — customers moving through a single review journey experienced a jarring shift in voice from one email to the next.
  • High perceived threat. Language led with restrictions, deadlines, and compliance obligations before explaining why the request existed, which made customers feel accused rather than protected.
  • Multiple back-and-forth cycles. Ambiguous instructions — especially around acceptable documentation — led to repeated rejections and resubmissions, extending resolution time and driving avoidable inbound support contacts.
Two

Research & diagnosis

  • Audited all ~30 macros against a shared rubric covering clarity, tone, actionability, and compliance accuracy, to identify which needed light edits versus a full rewrite.
  • Mapped the end-to-end customer journey across the most common review paths to pinpoint exactly where customers dropped off, got confused, or escalated to support.
  • Found that customers went through an average of 2.3 email rounds per case before submitting acceptable documentation — meaning most cases required at least one avoidable follow-up.
Three

Solution: a tone & voice framework

Rather than rewriting each template ad hoc, I defined five principles and applied them consistently across the full set — so the fix would scale and hold up under legal review:

  • Lead with customer protection, not investigation — every request was reframed around protecting the customer's account and identity, not investigating them.
  • Acknowledge the possibility of innocent error — simple lines like acknowledging the correct option may not have been selected at sign-up de-escalated defensiveness immediately.
  • Be action-driven — every email told customers exactly what to do next, with concrete examples of acceptable answers or documents.
  • Use plain language — compliance jargon and "regulated financial services company" boilerplate were removed in favor of plain, direct language.
  • Sign as the brand — emails were signed from the brand itself rather than "Compliance," removing the implied sense of being under investigation.

Each rewritten macro was reviewed with legal and compliance stakeholders to confirm no regulatory requirement or disclosure was lost — this was a tone and structure change only, not a change to what information was collected or why.

Four

Before / after

A representative example, reworded here to be generic rather than reproduce any actual template:

BEFORE
Subject: Resubmit required documentation
We have reviewed your submission and determined it does not meet our requirements. Please resubmit the correct documentation within the specified timeframe or your account may be restricted. Regards, Compliance Team
AFTER
Subject: We need a slightly different document
Thanks for sending that over — we just need one more version to complete this step. Here's exactly what qualifies, and a link to make it easy to upload. Your account stays fully active while we sort this out together. Kind regards, [Brand]

The shift from "you failed to comply" to "let's solve this together" was the core pattern applied across the full template set.

Five

Rollout

Changes were rolled out globally in a phased sequence, grouped by workflow (EDD documentation requests, anomaly investigation outreach, troubleshooting macros), with each batch tracked through standard change-management review before going live. Support and compliance teams were briefed ahead of each phase so frontline agents could set expectations with customers still in an open case.

Six

Results

Directionally, the redesign reduced friction across the review journey:

  • Resubmission rate roughly halved — customers submitted the correct documentation on the first attempt far more often.
  • Average resolution time dropped — cases resolved noticeably faster on average, reducing both customer wait time and case backlog.
  • Inbound support volume fell — fewer customers needed to contact support to understand what was being asked of them.
  • Customer sentiment improved — reply analysis suggested fewer hostile or confused responses, and more customers replying with the requested information directly.

All 30 templates were updated with zero changes to the underlying information requested or regulatory requirements — this was a tone and communication-design change, not a compliance-scope change.

Seven

What I'd carry forward

Reframing mandatory compliance communication from "instruction and threat" to "protection and clarity" measurably reduced customer friction without compromising regulatory requirements. Tone isn't a cosmetic layer on top of compliance messaging — it's a lever that directly affects response rates, resolution speed, and support load, and it's one compliance teams can use deliberately rather than as an afterthought.
